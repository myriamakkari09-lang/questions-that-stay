01 / THE BEGINNING

One ordinary drive.
A question I kept.

Stuck in traffic in Lebanon, I was mesmerized by a spotless Lamborghini. On my other side, three children were selling gum and water, asking people to buy from them. The contrast was difficult to ignore: two drastically different realities, just metres apart.

I kept wondering how one country could be so divided, and why some people struggled to meet basic needs while others lived with so much. Those questions widened into curiosity about businesses and government decisions. In ninth grade, I discovered that they all belonged to a field called economics.

The Secret History
Donna Tartt · Fiction
margin note: whose version are we hearing?

Through Richard Papen’s eyes, the people around him can seem almost mythic. But he is an unreliable narrator: he romanticizes, distorts, and tells events in a way that can make his choices easier to justify.

Reading him made me ask whether a story is the truth, or the version of it someone wants me to believe. I became fascinated by how the same person or event changes when the perspective changes. That habit of looking past the first account has followed me from books into films—and into the way I study economics.

Stuck in traffic, I was mesmerized by the spotless Lamborghini, driven by the beautiful blonde to my right. On my left, I was approached by three street children selling gum and water, desperately asking people to buy from them. The sight struck me. For the remainder of the trip, I couldn’t stop thinking about that moment. The contrast was very difficult to ignore. How could one country be so starkly divided? How could two drastically different realities exist just meters apart? Why were these children left struggling to meet their basic needs? Over time, my questions grew beyond wealth inequality. I became curious about how businesses operated and why governments made certain decisions, only to discover in ninth grade that these questions all belonged to a field called economics.

By nature, I tend to question everything around me, be it ideas, concepts, people, trends or events. This tendency was further nurtured by Donna Tartt’s The Secret History. Told through the eyes of Richard Papen, an unreliable narrator who romanticizes the people and atmosphere around him and distorts certain events in an attempt to justify his actions, I began to question whether what I was reading was the truth or simply the version of it he wanted the reader to believe. I became fascinated by how the same character, event, or idea could be interpreted differently based on who was analyzing it. This fascination soon extended beyond literature and into film, where I found myself rewatching scenes, noticing details and symbolism I had originally overlooked, and seeking out analyses that challenged my own interpretations. In turn, I became more open-minded, learning to consider every perspective beyond my own before forming an opinion. This way of thinking only deepened my interest in economics, a subject that gave me the opportunity to question why individuals, businesses, and governments make the decisions they do. It has also strengthened my approach to the subject, particularly when examining the reasons behind and the effects of economic policies and decisions. I take pleasure in considering why economists may interpret the same data differently and how an outcome that benefits one party may have drastically different consequences for another.

Although the sight of a Lamborghini juxtaposed with children begging no longer jolts me as it once did on that summer day, the fact that such a reality continues to exist in my home country and countless other developing nations remains unfathomable to me. I may have grown accustomed to witnessing such disparities, but I have never stopped questioning why they exist. I hope to pursue economics at university not only to better understand the world around me, but also to someday put that understanding to use in a career where the decisions I make can have a meaningful impact on the lives of others. Perhaps one day, I will be able to play even a modest role in changing the circumstances that once seemed so difficult for me to comprehend. The questions that began on an ordinary summer afternoon have stayed with me ever since, and I hope they will continue to guide the way I understand the world.
